A release check answers one question per aircraft: is it ready to fly today. It fails if a critical task or a functional check is overdue, if the last result on any task was a fail, or if the configuration does not match the baseline and a deviation has no note showing it does not affect the flight.

How this tracker works

Intervals tighten with life used. Each task can carry a life used and a life limit. Past 75 percent of life the interval is shortened to 75 percent of its base, and past 90 percent to 50 percent. This follows the idea in FAA AC 25-7D (paragraph 15.2.2.2) that inspection frequency increases as life is accumulated.

Functional checks count toward readiness. AC 25-7D (paragraph 4.4.9.2.5) treats the frequency of functional and ground maintenance checks as part of judging system reliability, so an overdue functional check blocks release here.

Configuration must match the baseline. AC 25-7D (paragraph 3.1.2.4, from 14 CFR 21.35) says a test airplane must conform to its type design as it relates to the test being flown, and any deviation must be shown to have no consequence for that test. Each aircraft here has a conformity flag and a deviation list with a required note.

AC 25-7D covers transport category airplanes. This tracker borrows its ideas for small UAS as a teaching example. It is not an approved maintenance program and does not replace the manufacturer's manual or your operator's procedures.
