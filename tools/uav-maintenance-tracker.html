How the checks are worked out

Intervals tighten as parts age. Past 75 percent of a part's life the interval drops to 75 percent of normal, and past 90 percent to half. This follows the idea in FAA AC 25-7D (paragraph 15.2.2.2) that inspections should get more frequent as life accumulates.

Functional checks count toward readiness. AC 25-7D (paragraph 4.4.9.2.5) treats the frequency of functional and ground checks as part of judging reliability, so an overdue functional check keeps an aircraft from being marked ready.

The aircraft has to match its baseline. AC 25-7D (paragraph 3.1.2.4, from 14 CFR 21.35) says a test aircraft must conform to its design for the test being flown, and any difference must be shown not to matter. Each aircraft has a baseline check and a list of differences that each need a note.

AC 25-7D covers transport category airplanes. I borrowed its ideas for small UAS as a working example. It is not an approved maintenance program and does not replace a manufacturer's manual or an operator's procedures.
